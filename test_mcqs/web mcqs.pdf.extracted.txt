What are the three core biophysical functions of air bubbles introduced in Segmented Flow Analysis
(SFA)?
A. Sample oxidation, foam reduction, and temperature stabilization
B. Segment partitioning, bolus flow mixing, and tubing scouring
C. Reagent precipitation, hydraulic acceleration, and protein coagulation
D. Photometric focusing, de-gasification, and viscosity reduction
Correct answer
• B. Segment partitioning, bolus flow mixing, and tubing scouring
Explanation In SFA, air bubbles injected at fixed intervals perform three distinct physical roles:
i. Segment Partitioning: Physical separation of the fluid stream into discrete liquid packets to
prevent axial dispersion and carryover between successive samples.
ii. Bolus Flow Friction & Mixing: Friction against the static inner tubing wall generates internal fluid
recirculation (bolus flow) within each segment, mixing samples with reagents.
iii. Tubing Scouring: The air-liquid interface wipes the internal lumen, sweeping away residual
droplets.
Why must air bubbles be purged by a debubbler module immediately prior to entering the flow cell in
a Segmented Flow Analyzer?
A. Air bubbles cause severe optical scattering and baseline signal noise
B. Air bubbles react chemically with chromogens, causing false discoloration
C. Air bubbles freeze the peristaltic pump rollers
D. Air bubbles denature the target enzymes inside the flow cell
Correct answer
• A. Air bubbles cause severe optical scattering and baseline signal noise
Explanation of the correct answer Because the refractive index of air differs dramatically from water, an
air bubble passing through a photometric light beam acts as a lens, scattering light and generating
massive voltage spikes and optical noise. Therefore, a T-shaped or Y-shaped glass debubbler or un-
segmented venting junction removes the air bubbles immediately upstream of the flow cell to present a
solid liquid column for measurement.
During a routine morning clinical chemistry run on an air-segmented continuous flow analyzer, the
technician notices that baseline photometer readings are drifting wildly and recorded sample peaks
show irregular, double-headed splits. What is the most likely root cause and corrective action?
A. The tungsten lamp has burned out; replace the light bulb immediately.

B. The air-injection manifold nipple is partially blocked, causing loss of uniform bubble
segmentation; clear the air injection port and check the air-to-liquid ratio.
C. The dialyzer membrane has ruptured; increase the peristaltic pump speed to maximum.
D. The reaction coil is too hot; turn off the heating bath cooling fan.
Correct answer
• B. The air-injection manifold nipple is partially blocked, causing loss of uniform bubble
segmentation; clear the air injection port and check the air-to-liquid ratio.
Explanation of the correct answer Uniform air bubble segmentation is required to maintain segment
integrity and prevent sample dispersion. If the air-injection port becomes partially obstructed, air
bubbles are introduced erratically or at improper sizes. This destroys bolus flow recirculation, allowing
adjacent liquid segments to bleed together (axial dispersion), which produces split, double-headed peaks
and severe carryover. Clearing the injection nipple and restoring correct manifold air-to-liquid ratios
resolves the issue.
According to IUPAC classification, what is the single defining characteristic that distinguishes discrete
analytical systems from continuous flow systems?
A. Physical transport and reaction of each sample or mixture within an individual, isolated container
B. Continuous movement of all specimens through a single shared hydraulic conduit
C. Exclusive reliance on air bubble segmentation to separate reaction zones
D. Inability to perform more than one test parameter per specimen
Correct answer
• A. Physical transport and reaction of each sample or mixture within an individual, isolated
container
Explanation of the correct answer Under the IUPAC classification scheme for automated analyzers, the
defining characteristic of discrete systems is discrete sample processing, where each individual sample
or reaction mixture is contained and transported within its own physically isolated container or cuvette.
Unlike continuous flow systems—where samples travel sequentially through a unified fluid stream—
discrete analyzers physically imitate manual test-tube procedures, preventing sample-to-sample reaction
vessel contamination. (Laboratory Organization. Automation (D.J. Vonderschmitt), Chapter 8, Section
8.1.2)
In automated discrete analyzers, what is the main purpose of monitoring pressure profiles via inline
pressure transducers during sample aspiration?
A. Measuring sample viscosity to calculate protein concentration
B. Detecting serum micro-clots or fibrin strands that cause aspiration failure
C. Regulating the temperature of the aspirated reagent

D. Determining the precise pH of the blood specimen
Correct answer
• B. Detecting serum micro-clots or fibrin strands that cause aspiration failure
Explanation of the correct answer Inline pressure transducers continuously monitor the vacuum profile
inside the sample probe fluidic line during aspiration. If a fibrin micro-clot or particulate matter partially
or fully obstructs the narrow probe orifice, the aspirating syringe creates an abnormal vacuum spike. The
system detects this abnormal pressure curve, flags the sample for clot error, aborts dispensing to prevent
cuvette contamination, and triggers an automated probe backflush/wash cycle.

Which primary optical technique is used to quantify chemical reactions on multi-layer dry-film slides
or test strips?
• A. Transmission turbidimetry
• B. Reflectance photometry (reflectometry)
• C. Fluorometry with 90° light scattering
• D. Atomic absorption spectrophotometry
Correct answer
• B. Reflectance photometry (reflectometry)
2. Explanation of the correct answer Dry chemistry platforms (e.g., Kodak Ektachem, Ortho VITROS,
Ames Seralyzer, Boehringer Reflotron) rely on reflectance photometry. Light from a monochromatic
source is directed through a transparent plastic support layer onto the opaque colored layer developed
within the dry slide matrix. The fraction of light diffusely reflected back is measured by a photodetector;
as analyte concentration increases, chromogen density rises, decreasing reflected light intensity.

In a multi-layer dry chemistry slide (e.g., Kodak Ektachem), what is the function of the top-most layer
containing titanium dioxide ($\text{TiO}_2$)?
• A. Generating an electrical current for potentiometric measurement
• B. Acting as a spreading layer that distributes the liquid sample drop uniformly and reflects light
• C. Binding colored dye indicator molecules
• D. Storing lyophilized enzyme reagents in buffer solution
Correct answer
• B. Acting as a spreading layer that distributes the liquid sample drop uniformly and reflects light
2. Explanation of the correct answer The top layer of a multi-layer dry-film slide is the spreading layer,
typically composed of porous cellulose acetate or polymer beads interspersed with titanium dioxide .It
serves two critical functions:
1. It causes a deposited drop of liquid specimen (6 to 11 $\mu\text{L}$) to spread rapidly and
isotropically by capillary action, ensuring a uniform volume per unit area.
2. The white $\text{TiO}_2$ matrix provides an opaque, highly reflective background that reflects
unabsorbed light back down toward the photodetector during reflectance photometry.
(Laboratory Organization. Automation (D.J. Vonderschmitt), Chapter 16, Section 3)

How do centrifugal analyzers achieve exact, simultaneous reaction initiation across all sample cuvettes
in a batch run?

• A. By using 30 individual robotic pipetting arms that dispense reagent into all cuvettes
simultaneously
• B. By holding sample and reagent in separate inner wells until rapid rotor acceleration propels all
liquids radially into outer cuvettes at the same instant
• C. By heating all cuvettes from 0°C to 37°C within 1 millisecond using laser pulses
• D. By passing an electric current through the rotor disc to trigger chemical activation
Correct answer
B. By holding sample and reagent in separate inner wells until rapid rotor acceleration propels all liquids
radially into outer cuvettes at the same instant
2. Explanation of the correct answer In a centrifugal analyzer, unmixed samples and reagents are pre-
pipetted into separate inner alveoles (wells) on the transfer rotor while stationary. When the analytical
run starts, the rotor accelerates rapidly to high speed. Centrifugal force instantly forces all sample and
reagent volumes outward through transfer channels into peripheral cuvettes simultaneously. Because
mixing occurs in every cuvette at the exact same fraction of a second, incubation and kinetic reaction
timing start in complete parallel across all samples.
Which sequence of high (H) and low (L) concentration sample pool analyses is standardly recommended
to experimentally detect and quantify sample-to-sample carryover on automated chemistry analyzers?
A. HLHLHLHL B. HHHLLLHHLLHHLL C. LLLHHHLLHH D. HHHHHLLLLL
Correct answer: B. HHHLLLHHLLHHLL
Explanation of the correct answer: Sample-to-sample carryover is evaluated experimentally by
analyzing high (H) and low (L) concentration sample pools in specific structured sequences, such as
$HHHLLLHHLLHHLL$. If carryover occurs, the low-concentration results immediately following a high-
concentration sample will show a statistically significant elevation compared to low-concentration
samples preceded by other low samples. (CLSI EP10)
(Tietz Fundamentals of Clinical Chemistry and Molecular Diagnostics, Chapter 16 (Automation), Passage
563.)
Automated sample probes detect the liquid surface level in primary collection tubes primarily through
which two physical sensing mechanisms?
A. Refractometry and turbidimetry B. Pressure and capacitive (radiofrequency) sensing C. Acoustic
ultrasonics and viscosity monitoring D. Optical density and fluorescence quenching
Correct answer: B. Pressure and capacitive (radiofrequency) sensing
Explanation of the correct answer: Liquid-level sensing on automated chemistry probes is typically
accomplished using either capacitive (radiofrequency) sensors—which detect changes in electrical
capacitance when the metal probe tip touches the liquid surface—or pressure sensors, which detect
changes in backpressure as the probe approaches or contacts the fluid.

In automated sampling, what is the term for the excess volume of serum or plasma that must remain in
a secondary sample cup to ensure the probe can aspirate the full required test volume without drawing
air? A. Dead volume B. Residual threshold C. Void volume D. Hold-up capacity
Correct answer: A. Dead volume
Explanation of the correct answer: Dead volume is the extra fluid volume that must be present at the
bottom of a sample cup or microtube above which the required test aliquot is aspirated. Short-sample
cups and false-bottom tubes are engineered to minimize dead volume.
